# The Neighbours

Open WebUI is the popular front door,
not the only one. An honest tour of
the street, so you can leave with
your eyes open if a neighbour suits
you better. (The backends are
neighbours too: Ollama has its own
guide in this series, and llama.cpp
stands behind half the doors below.)

- **Ollama's own interfaces.** The
  Ollama app and its terminal chat
  answer one person on one machine
  with zero setup. If accounts,
  history and documents are not your
  need, the simplest front door is
  the one you already have.
- **LibreChat.** The heavyweight
  alternative: multi-backend,
  multi-user, agents and plugins, a
  database stack to match. More
  capable in several directions
  than Open WebUI, more moving
  parts to keep. Houses that
  outgrow Open WebUI's assumptions
  usually land here.
- **Lobe Chat.** A polished,
  design-forward client with a
  plugin store and a lighter server
  story; pleasant for a single
  person driving many hosted APIs.
- **AnythingLLM.** Documents first:
  its workspaces-and-embeddings
  story is its own guide-shaped
  subject, with chat wrapped around
  it. If chapter 11 was your reason
  for reading, compare this one
  closely before settling.
- **LM Studio.** A desktop app for
  running models and chatting with
  them, which also serves the
  OpenAI dialect (chapter 7) — so it
  is both a neighbour and a possible
  backend behind Open WebUI itself.
- **text-generation-webui.** The
  tinkerer's workbench: every
  sampler knob exposed, model
  loading like a laboratory. A
  backend and an education in what
  the knobs do; not a family front
  door, and not trying to be.
- **The hosted chats themselves.**
  ChatGPT, Claude and friends need no
  install and carry models no home
  machine will match this year. What
  they don't offer is the subject of
  this whole guide: your accounts,
  your data, your documents, your
  door.

The deciding questions, in order:
must the words stay in the house?
must several people share one door?
do documents matter more than chat?
Answer those and the street usually
narrows to one or two houses — Open
WebUI wins the first two questions
often enough that its popularity is
not an accident.
